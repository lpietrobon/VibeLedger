# The spirit of this epic

“I want to see what all my investment accounts are worth, how much I put in or took out, and how much growth came from the investments themselves.” This includes after-tax brokerage and tax-sheltered Schwab/Fidelity accounts, viewed together or individually without becoming a professional trading terminal.

Investment visibility must not make everyday spending less trustworthy. Buying a fund is not shopping, a dividend is not a paycheck, and moving money from checking into a brokerage is not consumption. Keep distinct domains with explicit bridges for reconciliation and consolidated net worth. A hidden frontend filter is not sufficient separation.

Deliver useful truth in stages: verify coverage, retain reliable values and holdings, show balances, explain contributions versus gain, reveal pooled asset exposure, then earn the right to display percentage returns. Be explicit about what history exists. Missing information is not zero, and a plausible-looking percentage is not evidence.

Prefer one simple Investments destination, possibly occupying Overview's current navigation slot because Spending already covers much of that page. Preserve unique review and net-worth workflows rather than deleting them as collateral cleanup. Keep the first screen digestible, with details, evidence and methodology one action away.

Success means the user can explain a balance change and trust that neither their spending charts nor their investment returns have double-counted a transfer. The architecture and charts serve that understanding, not the other way around.
